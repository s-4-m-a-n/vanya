Vanya

Feature guide · Citizen-science biodiversity monitor

Vanya (वन्य) means “wild, of the forest”. Anyone can photograph wildlife, get an instant AI identification and help protect it. Every verified sighting becomes trustworthy data for conservation teams.

Part 1

The Vanya app

A phone-first web app (installable from the browser, no app store needed) for anyone who wants to photograph wildlife and help conservation.

Chapter 1

Getting started

Create an account, agree to share photo locations, and learn the safety rules.

Sign in or create an account

The welcome screen explains what Vanya does in three lines, then asks for an email and password. New users tap “New here? Create an account” and add their name.

  • Pick the language from the top-right menu: English, हिन्दी or Español. The choice is saved to the account.
  • Passwords are stored with Argon2 hashing; sessions use signed tokens.
  • Open the app on a phone and choose “Add to Home Screen” to install it like a native app.

Location consent and wildlife safety

Before the first photo, Vanya asks for consent to record where each photo was taken. Locations are what make a sighting useful for science, so this step is required.

The same screen sets the ground rules for responsible wildlife watching:

  • Keep your distance: zoom in instead of approaching.
  • Never disturb nests, dens or roosts.
  • Respect protected areas and private land.
Privacy promiseExact locations of threatened species are never shown publicly (see Chapter 6).
Chapter 2

Capturing a sighting

The Capture tab is the home screen: one big button to photograph what you have found.

The Capture screen

  • Greeting and prompt: “What did you spot today?”
  • Needs-your-attention banner: if any sighting is waiting for you to confirm its species, an amber banner says so. Tapping it opens just those sightings. The Sightings tab also shows a count badge.
  • Take photo: opens the phone camera. Vanya records the time and GPS position at that moment.
  • Choose from gallery: upload an earlier photo. Vanya reads the time and place saved inside the photo (EXIF), or you can say “I took this photo here, just now”.
  • Status pill (top right): Online, Offline or 2 to upload.
  • New users also see a short “How it works” card.

Adding details

  1. Check the location. The pill shows the GPS accuracy (for example “Location ±12 m”). If there is no GPS fix, the photo's own location is used when available.
  2. Pick a habitat with one tap: Forest, Grassland, Wetland, River or lake, Coast or sea, Desert or scrub, Mountain, Farmland, Town or garden or Other. Leave it on Auto to let Vanya decide.
  3. Add notes (optional), such as behaviour or numbers seen.
  4. Hide my exact location publicly (optional): others will only see a general area of about 20 km.
  5. Save sighting. Vanya uploads the photo and asks the AI to identify it, usually within seconds.
Scope of version 1Birds, mammals, reptiles, amphibians and plants. Insects, fish and fungi are recognised as “not covered yet” and will be added later.
Chapter 3

AI identification

Gemini looks at the photo and suggests the most likely species. The observer always makes the final pick.

“Is it one of these?”

After upload, the sighting opens with up to three suggestions, each with:

  • the common and scientific name and its IUCN Red List status (for example “Least concern” or “Endangered”);
  • a match bar showing the AI's confidence (here 91% Common Myna);
  • a short explanation of the features the AI used (“yellow bare skin around the eye…”).

Tap the right one to confirm it. If none fit, tap “Something else? Search species” and type a common or scientific name.

When the AI can't help

  • No animal, bird or plant visible → the app says so.
  • Species outside version 1 scope → “Not covered yet”.
  • AI unavailable → pick the species yourself with search.
Why the observer confirmsHuman confirmation plus AI confidence decide whether a record can be verified automatically (next chapter). Disagreeing with the AI is allowed; it just sends the record to an expert.
Chapter 4

Your sightings and verification

Every sighting moves through clear statuses, so observers always know where it stands.

The Sightings tab

  • Filter by All, Needs ID, In review or Verified; each filter shows a count.
  • Sightings that need you are highlighted in amber with “Tap to confirm the species”.
  • Each card shows the photo, species, status, rarity and the XP earned.
  • Dates read naturally: “Today, 10:12 AM”, “Yesterday, 9:12 AM”.
StatusMeaning
Confirm speciesWaiting for you to pick the species.
Checking…Automatic checks are running (seconds).
Awaiting expertAn expert must review it. Rare finds need two experts.
VerifiedAccepted: it counts for science and confirmed XP.
Not acceptedRejected, with the reason shown.

Sighting details

Opening a sighting shows the species, its IUCN status, and what happened during verification:

  • Verified automatically: a confident match (85% or more) for a species that is common in the area.
  • Verified by experts: expert notes and corrections are shown to the observer.
  • The XP card breaks the reward down (sighting, rarity bonus, new species for you, explorer bonus).
  • The facts card shows when and where it was seen, and how precisely the location is shown publicly.

What sends a record to experts

Low AI confidence · observer disagrees with the AI · threatened or extinct species claimed · outside its known range or uncommon locally · duplicate or near-duplicate photos · photo GPS or time doesn't match · screenshots, drawings, watermarks or captive animals.

Chapter 5

Rewards

XP rewards finding something new or rare, not just taking lots of photos.

The Me tab

  • Level and XP with a progress bar to the next level, plus XP still pending verification.
  • Stats: verified sightings, species, rare finds and reputation (how reliable your sightings have been).
  • Badges (12): First sighting, 10/50/100 species, Birder, Wildlife watcher, Botanist, Rare find, Conservation witness, Explorer, Regular and Trusted observer.
  • Leaderboards for this week, this month or all time, globally or near you (100 km).
  • Settings: language, show or hide me on leaderboards, link to the organisation dashboard (for members) and sign out.

XP is provisional while a sighting is in review and becomes confirmed once verified. Levels: Seedling, Sprout, Wanderer, Tracker, Naturalist, Field expert, Ranger, Guardian, Sentinel, Legend.

Rarity tiers

Rarity is calculated for a ~55 km area from GBIF records and Vanya's own verified data, combining how often the species is recorded locally, how widespread it is, and its conservation status.

Chapter 6

Protecting wildlife locations

Publishing the exact spot of a rare animal can help poachers. Vanya generalises locations automatically.

LevelWho gets itShown publicly as
OpenCommon speciesAbout 100 m precision
ObscuredThreatened species, unknown or unchecked status, or when the observer chooses “Hide my exact location”A ~20 km grid cell
HiddenSpecies an admin marks as highly sensitive (for example heavily poached)Not shown publicly at all
  • The stricter of the observer's choice and the species' sensitivity always wins.
  • Leaderboards, maps, dashboards and filters only ever use the generalised point.
  • The exact location can be revealed only by verifiers (while the record is waiting for review), analysts (verified records inside their organisation's regions) and platform admins, for a stated purpose (verification, research, management, enforcement). Every reveal is logged, with a limit of 100 per person per day.
  • The observer always sees their own exact location.

See the protected record in the review queue (Chapter 8) for how this looks to an expert.

Chapter 7

Offline, multilingual, day or night

Built for field work: poor signal, different languages and bright or dark conditions.

Offline: the sighting waits to upload
The app in Hindi

Works offline

With no signal, photos are saved on the phone with their time and GPS. The top bar shows Offline and the sighting appears as “Waiting to upload”. As soon as the phone is back online, Vanya uploads it and requests the AI identification automatically. Failed uploads can be retried or deleted.

Three languages

English, Hindi and Spanish today, including species names and AI answers in the user's language. More languages can be added without code changes to the screens.

Dark mode

Dark mode

Vanya follows the phone's light or dark setting automatically, which helps at dawn and dusk and saves battery on OLED screens.

Part 2 · Chapter 8

Expert review

Verifiers get a Review tab: a queue of sightings that automatic checks could not accept on their own.

Reviewing a sighting

  • Filters: by group (birds, animals, plants) and by area: within 100 km of your review area, or anywhere. “Set area to my location” saves your area.
  • The card shows the photo, the observer and their reputation, the time, habitat and notes.
  • Observer's identification next to the AI suggestions with confidence bars.
  • Why it needs review: the reasons, such as Low AI confidence.

Decisions

  • Confirm the observer's species.
  • It's a different species: search and confirm the correct one. The observer sees the correction.
  • Reject with a reason: captive or cultivated, wrong place or time, duplicate or not their photo, unidentifiable, no organism, wildlife disturbed, inappropriate.
  • Skip to the next record. An optional note goes back to the observer.

Rare records need two independent experts. Observer reputation, shown on the card, rises with verified sightings (+1 automatic, +2 expert, +5 rare) and falls when sightings are rejected (−3, or −10 for fraud).

Protected species in the queue

For a threatened species such as the tiger, the reviewer first sees only an approximate area with a Protected species label and a ★ Rare find badge.

If the exact spot is needed to judge the record, “Show exact location” asks for a purpose and reveals the device GPS and the photo's own GPS side by side. The reveal is written to an audit log.

Part 3

Organisation dashboard

A desktop dashboard at /dashboard for NGOs, park authorities and researchers. It shows only verified records inside the organisation's regions of interest.

Chapter 9

Overview and filters

  • Header: organisation name, unread alert count, language and a link back to the observer app.
  • Filters (one row, shared by every panel so the numbers always agree): last 30 days, 90 days, 12 months or all time; one region or all; birds, animals or plants. Clicking a species in “Most recorded species” filters everything to that species.
  • Headline numbers: verified sightings, species, observers, rare finds, threatened and invasive records.
Chapter 10

Distribution map and density grid

Sightings map

  • OpenStreetMap base with the organisation's regions outlined.
  • Points coloured by group: birds, animals, plants. A ring marks protected species, shown at their generalised position.
  • Click a point for the photo, species and date.
  • Side panels list the most recorded species and the rare and threatened records.
Density grid: ~55 km cells shaded by number of sightings
Chapter 11

Trends and changes

  • Sightings per month: a stacked chart by group. Hover a month for the exact numbers, or switch to a table view.
  • Biggest changes vs the previous period: species recorded more or less often than in the equal period before. The panel reminds readers that counts reflect observer effort too, so these are leads to investigate rather than population trends.
Chapter 12

Rare, invasive and new species

  • Invasive species (your watchlist): every verified record of species your organisation marked as invasive. Invasiveness depends on place, so each organisation keeps its own list.
  • New to your regions: species recorded for the first time in your regions during the period.
  • Rare and threatened (Chapter 10): records with a high rarity tier or a threatened IUCN status.
Chapter 13

Alerts

Vanya raises an alert as soon as a sighting is verified, typically within a minute, when it is:

  • Invasive: a species on your invasive watchlist;
  • Priority species: a species on your priority watchlist;
  • New record: the first verified record of a species in your regions.

Alerts show the region and how quickly they were raised. Team members mark them as seen; the header shows the unread count. Alerts can also be pushed to your own systems with a signed webhook (Chapter 16).

Chapter 14

Ask the analyst

Ask questions in plain language, for example:

  • “Which species were recorded less often in the last 3 months than before?”
  • “Top 5 birds in the last 90 days”
  • “How many invasive species sightings this year, by month?”

The analyst (Gemini) restates how it understood the question, answers with key points and caveats, and “Show the data” reveals the exact figures behind the answer. Recent questions are kept for the team.

Safe by designThe AI never writes database queries. It picks from a fixed set of validated, parameterised queries, only sees aggregated numbers from your organisation's verified records, and is instructed to answer only from that data.
Chapter 15

Reports

One click writes a weekly, monthly or quarterly report for your regions: a summary, sections on overall activity, rare and threatened species, invasive species, new records and notable changes, and practical recommendations.

  • Reports are saved and listed in the Reports panel.
  • Print or save as PDF produces a clean document for funders, boards or park managers.
  • Reports can also be generated for every organisation on a schedule.

The model returns structured content that is displayed as plain text, so nothing from the AI can inject formatting or code into the page.

Chapter 16

Managing your organisation

Organisation admins configure what the dashboard covers and who can see it. Analysts can view everything but not change settings.

Regions of interest

Draw a region by clicking corners on the map, or upload a GeoJSON file (for example a park boundary). The area is shown in km². All panels, alerts and reports are limited to these regions.

Watchlist

Search any species and add it as Invasive or Priority. The watchlist drives the invasive panel and alerts.

Members

Add colleagues by email as Admin or Analyst, or remove them.

Alert settings and webhooks

Choose which alert types to receive and add an optional HTTPS webhook. Each alert is POSTed as JSON and signed with HMAC-SHA256 in the X-Vanya-Signature header. Admins can create a new signing secret and send a test alert.

Data sources and technology

Species identification and analysis: Google Gemini. Taxonomy, range and rarity baselines: GBIF. Conservation status: IUCN Red List (via GBIF). Maps: OpenStreetMap and Leaflet. The platform runs on open-source components locally and is designed to move to Google Cloud (Cloud Run, Cloud SQL, Cloud Storage, Pub/Sub, BigQuery and Vertex AI) without changing features.
